## Resulting behavior

The previous disabled foundation accepted an evidence UUID after current
administrator/MFA and order-grant checks. It did not bind actual receipt details.
The new TypeScript and SQL contract requires recipientId, receiptReference,
amountMinor, ZMW currency, evidenceReference and explicit reviewer attestation.
Both commercial and manual-payment settings must be enabled; switching settings
off cannot restore UUID-only confirmation.

The database repeats current session/MFA, role and unexpired per-order grant
checks under the existing administration lock. Customer, vendor owner and vendor
membership conflicts deny review, including inactive membership. Recipient,
active vendor, accepted quote and immutable commercial amount/currency must
match. Partial, excess, wrong-recipient and malformed claims leave the order unpaid.

Locale-independent ASCII provider/reference hashes and unique private evidence references stop
cross-order duplicates; concurrent equivalent references pay exactly one order.
An immutable receipt binds reviewer/session, time, recipient, order, quote,
amount/currency and the same committed workflow audit. Audit outage rolls back
both the receipt and payment state. Exact authorized retries reuse the original
result; changed retries conflict. A legacy payment UUID without a matching receipt
cannot advance an order, even after settings are disabled.

Both new tables have forced RLS and no direct runtime/browser grants. The
reconciliation helper has no service-role execution grant. Recipient identity
and receipt history are immutable; private operators may enable/disable an
existing recipient under the administration lock. No public configuration or
alternative payment route is added. Raw transfer references are not retained
in receipts, returned in responses or added to ordinary audit results. Digests
are not encryption or anonymization.

This records an authorized human's independently checked receipt; it is not an
automatic bank verification service. A screenshot, evidence UUID or checkbox
alone does not establish settlement. Dishonest/compromised authorized reviewers,
private evidence availability and actual bank reconciliation remain operating
risks. Customer payment remains separate from commission received and vendor
settlement; completion accrues the frozen commission once.

## Security evidence and retained failures

The payment threat requires an authenticated privileged reviewer with an active
order grant; this was an incomplete receipt gate, not a demonstrated anonymous
or hosted authorization bypass. Reused or mismatched evidence could previously
mark an unrelated amount as paid. The new SQL constraints and transaction checks
deny those local cases. An operator must still verify actual bank receipt and
approve provider reference uniqueness/normalization before live use.

The fresh full audit exits nonzero for minimatch 10.2.6:
[maintainer advisory GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249](https://github.com/isaacs/minimatch/security/advisories/GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249).
It describes pattern-amplification denial of service at optimizationLevel >= 2;
the published fix is 11.0.1. Source inspection finds no minimatch import in the
application/scripts/config or optimizationLevel option in the reviewed ESLint
consumer directories. Request-path reachability and an exploit are not
reproduced. The moderate finding remains open for a compatible toolchain update.
The audit's proposed forced ESLint downgrade to 4.0.0 and a blind major override
are not applied. No high/critical finding is reported in this root audit; this
does not clear the separate retained hosting/runtime release blocker.

Initial sandbox tests cannot start PostgreSQL and fail two existing subprocess
checks; authorized local reruns pass. The recipient fixture first needs an
explicit UUID parameter cast, then fails an existing provisioning-audit guard
when adding a conflict-of-interest membership. The fixture now supplies the
required same-transaction audit; application guards remain intact. The final
complete replay passes afterward. A registry-bound repeat install stalls; only
its verified task-owned process is stopped, then the unchanged lock installs
from cache. Both fresh advisory checks run separately. No forced dependency
downgrade, hosted database change or broad process cleanup occurs.

All probes use synthetic loopback fixtures. Credentials, raw logs, real account
details/customer files/vendor evidence and signed URLs stay out of Git/reports.
Tool/document output is treated as untrusted; no instruction injection, secret
interpolation or unsupported privilege expansion is accepted. This is a scoped
application review, not a provider/OS/workstation penetration test.
